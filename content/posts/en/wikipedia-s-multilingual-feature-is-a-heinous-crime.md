These past couple of days I’ve been trying to find a good name for something I’m making, so I searched Google for names of various things and jumped to Wikipedia to browse entries (a very weird way to name things).

What made me lose my mind was that I landed on a page that felt completely unfamiliar. It wasn’t Wikipedia’s default white-background page, but a beige-background page. 

Then I looked closer, and the URL was ~~fucking~~

> zh-classical.wikipedia.org 

instead of

> zh.wikipedia.org

And the upper left corner prominently said “典 大 基 维”

![维基大典](https://cloudflarecnimg.scdn.io/i/6b0168e93a9cb_1795254505.png)

The body text was written entirely in Classical Chinese.

I was TOTALY FUCKING confused: would anyone in the 21st century still use Classical Chinese for daily communication???

So the mysterious Wikipedia community adding Classical Chinese to various entries is probably not because one of them is trying to become a member of an ancient Chinese feudal dynasty, or because someone activated some kind of system from ancient China that transmigrated them into the modern era, where writing an encyclopedia in Classical Chinese makes them stronger, and then they came here via transmigration.

After thinking for a long time, I roughly figured out that what probably drove these weirdos to do this was a desire to imitate Minecraft.

That’s right, Minecraft has all kinds of languages, including:

 - Classical Chinese
 - lolcat
 - Shakespearean English
 - Reversed English
 - Pirate English
 
But!

Minecraft is a game. Adding a bunch of made-up bullshit languages to it does no harm.

Wikipedia is a serious encyclopedia. Its About page (Chinese) even says:
 

> provides knowledge in all fields for its readers

So it’s a site that provides knowledge, right?

WHO THE FUCK reads Wikipedia every day in Classical Chinese, Wu Chinese, or Hakka?

I don’t oppose the existence of entries in these languages, but search results show up in these very uncommon languages almost more often than in common languages.

To read an entry, I first have to do a translation inside my head into zh-cn or en-us or some other language humans can directly understand. It’s a completely anti-human design.

Especially dialects—you even have to read them out loud to know what they mean.

The quality of these entries is also far lower than in other commonly used languages, and their practical value is almost zero.

THE DUMBEST FUCKING PART is that many results Google grabs are pages in languages that a human trying to obtain information and knowledge normally shouldn’t be reading, so when I open these shitty entries I’m completely baffled, and then I spend a huge amount of time switching to a normal language.

Although there are some ways to avoid this situation, but...

Who would have thought Wikipedia would have these languages, and that Google would show you results in them?

*A multilingual feature originally meant to make things convenient for people who use different languages becomes a disaster when used inappropriately.*
